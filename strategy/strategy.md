# Google Ads strategy — SketchRoot

## Primary goal
Generate qualified visits and sign-ups for SketchRoot's dental learning resources. Choose the primary conversion only after confirming the current signup and product flow.

## Audience hypotheses to validate
1. Dental students looking for visual, structured study resources.
2. General dentists and specialists pursuing continuing learning.
3. Dental licensing-exam aspirants, where the platform has relevant, verified content.
4. Educators or institutions evaluating dental-learning resources.

These are starting hypotheses, not confirmed audience performance.

## Recommended phased rollout

### Phase 0 — readiness
- Confirm the live product, available subjects, pricing, signup process, and geographic availability.
- Review landing pages on mobile and desktop.
- Implement analytics and conversion tracking.
- Use Keyword Planner and Google Trends to validate search demand.

### Phase 1 — high-intent Search
Start with tightly themed ad groups based on verified product offerings, for example:
- Dental study resources
- Dental visual learning
- Dental exam preparation (only if the product supports the named exam)
- Brand searches for SketchRoot

Keep brand and non-brand traffic separate. Do not mix unrelated intents in one ad group.

### Phase 2 — controlled expansion
After sufficient conversion data and landing-page QA:
- Test additional subject-specific terms.
- Test remarketing only if consent, audience eligibility, and platform policies permit.
- Consider video or Demand Gen only when creative and measurement are ready.

## Budgeting
Set the budget based on the owner's approved test amount, not an assumed industry benchmark. Start small enough to learn safely; adjust only after evaluating qualified conversions, cost per acquisition, and lead/signup quality.

## Optimization principles
- Search terms and negatives reviewed frequently during launch.
- Evaluate conversion rate and qualified conversion cost, not CTR alone.
- Separate brand/non-brand and materially different geographies.
- Change one major variable per experiment where practical.
- Record dates, hypotheses, changes, and outcomes.
